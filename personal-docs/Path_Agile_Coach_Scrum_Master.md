# Agile Coach & Scrum Master Role Guide

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

A role guide for **Scrum Master** and **Agile Coach** interviews with engineering
teams, including AI and data teams. You help teams and organisations work
effectively: Scrum and Kanban done well, facilitation, coaching and removing
barriers. This page covers the role, the usual loop, the rubric, the likely
questions, and a 14-day plan. Scrum terms follow the 2020 Scrum Guide.

!!! abstract "What this role is really testing"
    **Can you help a team get better at delivering value, and can you show it?**
    Real understanding of Scrum and flow (not just the vocabulary), strong
    facilitation, a coaching stance, and the courage to address organisational
    problems. Interviewers look for evidence of change you helped create, not
    certificates.

    Running cross-team delivery with dates and dependencies? See the
    [Technical Program Manager guide](Path_Technical_Program_Manager.md).

---

## What the role is in 2026

Teams still need someone who helps them improve how they work, and organisations
still struggle to change. Interviewers now probe harder on **outcomes**: what got
better because you were there. With AI and data teams, there are two extra
questions:

- **Uncertain work.** How Sprints, goals and Definition of Done work when an
  experiment may fail.
- **Flow over ceremony.** Many data and platform teams run Kanban or a hybrid,
  so flow metrics and work-in-progress limits come up often.

| | Scrum Master | Agile Coach | Delivery or project manager |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | One or a few Scrum Teams | Teams, leaders and the wider organisation | Plans, dates and scope |
| **Main tools** | Scrum events, facilitation, impediment removal | Coaching, training, organisational change | Plans, status, risk logs |
| **Judged on** | Team effectiveness and self-management | Lasting change across the organisation | Delivery against plan |
| **Guide** | This page | This page | [TPM guide](Path_Technical_Program_Manager.md) |

## The interview loop by company type

These are **common patterns**, not a description of any one company's current
process. Ask whether there is a facilitation exercise and who you will meet.

### Product or tech company

Often a conversation with the hiring manager, a session with a team you would
support, a facilitation exercise (for example running a short retrospective with
the interviewers), and a round with engineering or product leaders.

### Enterprise or transformation program

More focus on scaling, leadership coaching and change management. Expect
questions on frameworks such as SAFe, LeSS or Nexus, and on working with
leadership who want quick results.

### Consulting

Expect case-style questions: "A client's teams miss every commitment. What do
you do in your first month?" Presentation skills and handling resistance matter.

## Scoring rubric: what "strong hire" sounds like

| Area | Strong hire | Weak signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrum and flow** | Explains the purpose behind each event and artifact; adapts to context | Recites the guide; dogmatic |
| **Facilitation** | Clear structure, every voice heard, ends with decisions | Talks most of the time |
| **Coaching** | Asks good questions; grows team ownership | Solves every problem for the team |
| **Metrics** | Uses flow metrics with the team to improve | Uses velocity to compare teams |
| **Organisation** | Names systemic impediments and works on them with leaders | Only works inside the team |
| **Evidence** | Concrete before and after | "The team became more agile" |

## Common failure modes

- **Reciting the Scrum Guide** without explaining why the rules exist or when
  you would adapt.
- **Acting as team secretary.** Booking meetings and updating the board is not
  the job.
- **No evidence.** Claims of improvement with nothing observable behind them.
- **Framework fan.** Recommending a heavy scaling framework before trying simple
  coordination.
- **Blaming management** without describing what you did to work with them.

## 14-day plan

| Day | Focus | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Add the job in OfferReady. Answer questions 1, 5 and 13 out loud; note the gaps. |
| 2 | Scrum Guide | Re-read the 2020 Scrum Guide. Explain each event's purpose in one sentence. |
| 3 | Foundations | Questions 1–5 with an example from your own teams. |
| 4 | Flow | Questions 6–8. Prepare one story where flow metrics changed a decision. |
| 5 | Facilitation | Plan a 30-minute retrospective you could run live in an interview. |
| 6 | Coaching | Questions 9–12. Practise asking questions instead of giving answers. |
| 7 | Mock 1 | Voice mock. List every follow-up you struggled with. |
| 8 | Organisation | Questions 13–15. Know the trade-offs of at least two scaling approaches. |
| 9 | AI and data teams | Questions 16–18. |
| 10 | Story bank | Six stories: success, failure, resistance, conflict, a leader you coached, a metric that helped. |
| 11 | Weak spots | Rewrite the two answers that went worst in mock 1. |
| 12 | Company | Learn how the company builds products; prepare questions for them. |
| 13 | Mock 2 | Voice mock with Deep follow-ups on. |
| 14 | Light review | Re-read your story bank and [Cheat Sheets](Interview_Cheat_Sheets.md), then rest. |

## Are you ready? (self-check)

- [ ] I can explain why each Scrum event and artifact exists, not just what it is.
- [ ] I can run a short retrospective live, with a clear structure.
- [ ] I have a story with an observable before and after.
- [ ] I can use flow metrics to discuss improvement and forecasting.
- [ ] I can describe how I would work with a sceptical leader.
- [ ] I can explain how Scrum or Kanban works for an AI or data team.
