# AI & Technical Product Manager Role Guide

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

A role guide for **AI Product Manager** and **Technical Product Manager**
interviews. You decide what to build and why, for products where AI or deep
technology is central: AI features, developer platforms, data products. This
page covers the role, the usual loop, the rubric, the likely questions, and a
14-day plan.

!!! abstract "What this role is really testing"
    **Can you find the right problem, decide what to build, and prove it worked,
    when the technology is probabilistic and costly?** Product sense, metrics,
    prioritisation and working with engineering, plus AI-specific judgement:
    when AI is the right tool, how quality is evaluated, what happens when it is
    wrong, and what it costs.

    Owning delivery across teams rather than the product decisions? See the
    [Technical Program Manager guide](Path_Technical_Program_Manager.md).

---

## What the role is in 2026

Product management fundamentals have not changed: understand users, choose
problems, define success and ship. AI products add questions interviewers now
ask directly:

- **Is AI the right tool?** A simpler rule-based solution sometimes wins.
- **What does "good" mean?** Quality is measured on evaluation sets and in
  production signals, and the PM defines which mistakes are acceptable.
- **What happens when it is wrong?** Design for errors: visible, recoverable,
  with humans in the loop where stakes are high.
- **What does it cost?** Model usage affects pricing and margins.

| | AI / technical PM | Core product PM | Technical program manager |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owns** | What to build and why, for AI or technical products | What to build and why | Delivery across teams |
| **Users** | End users, or developers for platform products | End users | Internal teams |
| **Judged on** | Product outcomes, quality, cost | Product outcomes | Program delivered, risk managed |
| **Guide** | This page | — | [TPM guide](Path_Technical_Program_Manager.md) |

For a non-technical product role, add the job in OfferReady: the plan is built
from that job description.

## The interview loop by company type

These are **common patterns**, not a description of any one company's current
process. Ask the recruiter which case types to expect and whether there is a
technical round.

### Big tech

Typically product sense (design a product), analytical or metrics, execution or
prioritisation, a technical round for technical PM roles, and behavioural or
leadership rounds.

### AI-native startup

Expect a product exercise built around the company's own product, a deep
conversation about evaluation and model behaviour, and founder rounds about
speed and judgement. A take-home such as a short spec is common.

### Enterprise software

More focus on customers, sales and integration: discovery with enterprise
buyers, security and compliance requirements, and roadmaps shaped by large
accounts.

## Scoring rubric: what "strong hire" sounds like

| Area | Strong hire | Weak signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Product sense** | Clear user and problem, prioritised solution, honest trade-offs | Jumps to features |
| **Metrics** | One primary outcome, quality measures and guardrails | A long list of metrics |
| **Prioritisation** | Tied to goals, explicit about what is not done | Everything is important |
| **AI judgement** | Knows when not to use AI; designs for errors; owns the quality bar | "Add an LLM" to everything |
| **Technical fluency** | Discusses architecture, cost and constraints credibly | Avoids technical questions |
| **Collaboration** | Partners with engineering and design; examples of resolved disagreements | "I told the engineers" |

## Common failure modes

- **Solution first.** Starting with "we add a chatbot" before naming the user
  and the problem.
- **Metric soup.** Many metrics with no primary one and no guardrails.
- **Ignoring failure cases.** No plan for when the AI is wrong.
- **Hand-waving on cost.** No sense of what the feature costs to run.
- **Vague stories.** "We launched and it went well" with no numbers you can
  stand behind or no learning.

## 14-day plan

| Day | Focus | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Add the job in OfferReady. Answer questions 2, 5 and 9 out loud; note the gaps. |
| 2 | Product sense | Questions 1–4. Practise one design question with a clear structure. |
| 3 | Metrics | Questions 5–7. For one product you know, write a primary metric and two guardrails. |
| 4 | AI basics | [GenAI Q&A](GenAI_Interview_QA.md): how models, retrieval and evaluation work at a high level. |
| 5 | AI product work | Questions 8–10. |
| 6 | Build vs buy, safety | Questions 11–13. Draft a one-page spec for an AI feature. |
| 7 | Mock 1 | Voice mock with a product design round. List every follow-up you missed. |
| 8 | Collaboration | Questions 14–16 with real stories. |
| 9 | Launch | Question 17. Write a staged launch plan with stop criteria. |
| 10 | Strategy | Question 18 for the company you are interviewing with. |
| 11 | Story bank | Six stories: launch, failure, disagreement, saying no, a metric decision, a user insight. |
| 12 | Company | Use the company's product; note three improvements and how you would measure them. |
| 13 | Mock 2 | Voice mock with Deep follow-ups on. |
| 14 | Light review | Re-read your notes and [Cheat Sheets](Interview_Cheat_Sheets.md), then rest. |

## Are you ready? (self-check)

- [ ] I can structure a product design answer from user to launch.
- [ ] I can choose a primary metric and guardrails for an AI feature.
- [ ] I can explain when I would not use AI.
- [ ] I can describe how quality is evaluated and who sets the bar.
- [ ] I can discuss the cost of an AI feature and the levers to reduce it.
- [ ] I have an honest story about a product decision that did not work.
