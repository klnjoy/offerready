# Free courses, in order

*Last reviewed: October 2026*

There are hundreds of free AI courses. You need a few, in the right order.
Pick the stage you're at and take one course from it before moving on. Every
link below is free (some Coursera courses are free to audit; you pay only for
a certificate).

!!! tip "Learning for an interview?"
    Courses build understanding; interviews test whether you can explain and
    defend it. After each stage, open the matching topic in this library and
    practise its questions in the
    [OfferReady app](https://getofferready.app/practice/bank).

## Stage 1 · First concepts (no code)

| Course | Who | Why take it |
|---|---|---|
| [Elements of AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/) | University of Helsinki | What AI is and isn't, in plain language. A few evenings. |
| [AI for Everyone](https://www.coursera.org/learn/ai-for-everyone) | DeepLearning.AI | How AI projects work in a company. Free to audit. |
| [Neural networks](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/neural-networks) | 3Blue1Brown (videos) | The clearest visual explanation of how networks and transformers learn. |

## Stage 2 · Python and data

| Course | Who | Why take it |
|---|---|---|
| [Python](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/python) | Kaggle | Just enough Python, in your browser. |
| [All Kaggle Learn courses](https://www.kaggle.com/learn) | Kaggle | Pandas, data cleaning and visualisation, each a few hours. |
| [CS50's Intro to AI with Python](https://cs50.harvard.edu/ai/) | Harvard | Search, logic, ML and neural nets, with real projects. |

## Stage 3 · Machine learning foundations

| Course | Who | Why take it |
|---|---|---|
| [Intro to Machine Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intro-to-machine-learning) | Kaggle | Train and validate your first models. |
| [Machine Learning Crash Course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course) | Google | The core ideas with exercises. |
| [Practical Deep Learning](https://course.fast.ai/) | fast.ai | Build working models first, theory second. |
| [CS229 Machine Learning](https://cs229.stanford.edu/) | Stanford | The maths, for when you want depth. |

## Stage 4 · LLMs and generative AI

| Course | Who | Why take it |
|---|---|---|
| [LLM Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/llm-course) | Hugging Face | Transformers, tokenizers and fine-tuning, chapter by chapter. |
| [Learn Prompting](https://learnprompting.org/) | Learn Prompting | Prompting techniques from basic to advanced. |
| [Short courses](https://www.deeplearning.ai/short-courses/) | DeepLearning.AI | One-hour courses on RAG, evals and agents, often with the tool makers. |
| [Anthropic Academy](https://anthropic.com/learn) | Anthropic | Claude, the API, MCP and Claude Code. |
| [Vector database learning](https://zilliz.com/learn) | Zilliz | Embeddings, indexes and vector search explained. |

## Stage 5 · Agents and production

| Course | Who | Why take it |
|---|---|---|
| [AI Agents Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/agents-course) | Hugging Face | Build agents with tools, memory and evaluation. |
| [Introduction to LangGraph](https://academy.langchain.com/) | LangChain Academy | Agents as graphs: state, memory, human-in-the-loop. |
| [MLflow docs](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/) | MLflow | Tracking experiments, models and LLM traces. |
| [Deep RL Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/deep-rl-course) | Hugging Face | Reinforcement learning, if your role needs it. |

## Then: prove it

Courses alone don't get offers. For each stage, build one small thing (a
notebook, a RAG demo, an agent with two tools), write down one decision you
made and why, and practise explaining it. Start with
[Agentic AI in 7 diagrams](../agent-diagrams/index.md) to learn to draw and
explain the core designs.
