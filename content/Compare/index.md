# Compare OfferReady

There are three common ways to use AI for interview prep, and one well-known
way without it: mock interviews with real engineers. Here is what each one is
good at, so you can pick what fits you.

Many live assistants also offer practice mock interviews. The table compares
their *live* mode, which is what sets them apart.

| | General chatbot | Live interview assistant (live mode) | OfferReady |
|---|---|---|---|
| **When you use it** | Any time | *During* the real interview | Before the interview |
| **Knows your target job** | Only if you paste it in every time | Partly | Yes: saved per job, with requirements and gaps |
| **Answers built from your resume** | Can make up details | Generated live | Yes, and every point is labelled with where it came from |
| **Flags made-up numbers** | No | No | Yes: numbers not found in your material are flagged |
| **Grades your answer** | Opinion | Not in live mode | A rubric, with a quote from your answer behind every score |
| **Shows that you improved** | No | Not in live mode | Retry and compare: before and after, criterion by criterion |
| **Follow-up pressure** | If you ask | Not in live mode | Trade-off drills and mocks that follow up on what you said |
| **Risk with employers** | None | Can break interview rules, and you may be disqualified if detected | None: practice only |

## Live interview assistants

Some tools advertise real-time help during live interviews, including
modes designed not to show up on screen share. OfferReady deliberately doesn't do this:

- Many employers forbid outside help in interviews, and candidates who are
  caught are removed from the process.
- An answer read off a screen falls apart at the first follow-up question,
  and senior interviews are mostly follow-up questions.
- Your next job depends on being able to do the work, not just on getting
  through the interview.

We give you the same speed while you *practise*: instant answer outlines
from your own experience, likely follow-ups, and examples from your resume.
You then rehearse until you can say the answer yourself.

## Human mock interviews

Human mock interview platforms give you something OfferReady can't: a
mock interview with a senior, staff or principal engineer from a large tech
company, with written feedback, a recording, a live coding environment and,
for strong performers, possible referrals. They also offer an AI interviewer
for coding and system design.

| | Human mock platform | OfferReady |
|---|---|---|
| **Who interviews you** | A real senior engineer | An AI interviewer that follows up on what you said |
| **Starts from your target job** | No: practice by interview type | Yes: the job's requirements, your resume match and gaps |
| **Plan to your interview date** | No | Day by day, with a forecast ready date |
| **Answers from your own stories** | Your own preparation | AI Interview Coach, every point labelled with its source |
| **Live coding environment** | Yes | Not yet: coding concepts are in the question bank |
| **After the real interview** | No | Interview notes, then a debrief that feeds your plan |
| **Roles** | Strongest for software engineering | AI, data, platform, forward-deployed and architect roles |
| **Price** | Often over $100 a session | Free to start; one-time passes from $19 |

**Use both.** Prepare every day with OfferReady, then book one or two human
mocks when your readiness says you're close, so the expensive sessions test
an answer you've already practised rather than teach you the basics.

## When a general chatbot is enough

If you need one concept explained, a chatbot is great. OfferReady is for when
you have a real interview coming up and want a plan, honest feedback and
proof that you're improving.
