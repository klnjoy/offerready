# Practice

Four ways to practise. Pick what fits; you don't need all four. You can switch
between them any time, and your progress is kept.

| Tool | Use it when | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Questions for this job** | You want the likely questions for this interview | Questions written from the job and your gaps. Answer, get rubric feedback, try again. |
| **Trade-off drills** | The role involves design decisions | Make a call on a real scenario, then hold it while the interviewer pushes on cost, limits and incidents. |
| **Your stories** | Behavioral rounds | Write STAR stories, matched to what the job asks for, with AI coaching on each one. |
| **Question bank** | Quick reps on a topic | Answer and rate yourself, flashcards, a timed exam, or your weakest topics. |

## Leaving in the middle

In a drill, **Save & exit** keeps your place and **Finish now** ends it and
scores what you've done so far. Practice tabs keep your progress when you
switch between them.

## How answers are graded

Each answer is scored against a rubric for its type of question (system design,
data pipelines, behavioral and so on). For every criterion you see:

- a rating, from missing to strong;
- **a quote from your answer** as the evidence (no quote, no credit);
- a short note on what would make it stronger.

Very short answers, and answers missing the key criterion (for example the
trade-off in a design question), are capped, so a high score means something.
Each result also has a **staff upgrade**: the one thing that would lift the
answer a level.

## Try again and compare

After feedback, choose **Try again and compare**. The new score and every
criterion that changed are shown side by side, for example
"Attempt 2 vs attempt 1: 42% → 61%" and "Vague → Solid". Save your best
version to **Your answers**.
