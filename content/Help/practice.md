# Practice

Open **Practice** in the top bar. Every practice page has the **practice path**
at the top:

| Step | Tool | Done when you've… |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Questions for this job** | answered 5 questions for this job |
| **2** | **Trade-off drill** | finished one drill |
| **3** | **Your stories** | written one STAR story for this job |
| **4** | **Mock interview** | finished one mock interview |
| Any time | **Question bank** | (no goal: quick reps whenever you like) |

A finished step gets a green tick. Under the steps, one line tells you where
you are and what's next:

- **"Step 2 of 4: hold one design call under push-back. Then step 3, Your
  stories. Skip to Your stories"**: you're on this step. **Skip to …** moves
  on without doing it.
- **"Step 2 done. Next: Your stories"** with a **Go to Your stories** button:
  this step is done and the next one is highlighted.

The steps are a **suggestion, not a gate**. Skip any you don't need: for
example, skip trade-off drills for a role with no design round. You can
switch tools any time, and each one keeps your progress.

## The Practice page

**Practice** lists each way to practise once, with where you are on it:

- **Ways to practise:** Questions for this job, Trade-off drills, Your
  stories, Mock interview and the Question bank. Each row shows its status:
  **Next** (with progress, such as *1/5*), **Step 2**, **Done**, or for the
  question bank your **weakest topic**, which opens the bank on that topic.
- **Learn AI:** a row of cards you can swipe, with short reads from the study
  library (how LLMs work, prompts, RAG, vector databases, agents, MCP,
  evaluation, deployment). Topics your job mentions come first, marked
  **In your job**.

The AI Coach has its own tab, so it isn't repeated here.

## 1. Questions for this job

Likely interview questions written from the job description and the gaps in
your resume, saved to the job.

1. Open **Questions for this job**. The job at the top is your current job.
   Use **Switch job** to change it.
2. Choose **Write my questions**. About 16 questions are written in roughly
   15 seconds; **Get more questions** adds more that don't repeat them.
   Optional: **Edit the job description first**.
3. Filter by **Not practised**, **Technical**, **Behavioral**, **System
   design** or **Leadership**, or choose **Practise the next one**.
4. Open a question. **Say it out loud first**, then type the main points. Use
   **Start 2-minute timer** to keep it to interview length.
5. Choose **Get feedback**. You see your score, what the interviewer listens
   for, what a strong answer says, and the follow-up they'd ask next.
6. **Try again and compare**, or **Answer the follow-up**.

**Get more questions** adds new ones and keeps the ones you've practised.
**Why these questions** shows which skills and gaps they came from.

### Who's interviewing you?

Each kind of interviewer asks different things. Under **Who's interviewing
you?** pick **Recruiter / HR**, **Hiring manager**, **Technical peer**,
**Director / VP** or **Business partner** to see what they care about and how
to answer. Then choose **Get their likely questions** for about 8 questions
that person is likely to ask for this job, each with why they ask and how to
answer, and **Practise with AI Coach**.

You can add **Their title** (for example "Director of Data Platform") to make
the questions closer. Only the interviewer's role and your job are used; we
don't look anyone up. The questions are kept on this device for each job.

## 2. Trade-off drills

You make a design call on a real scenario, then hold it while the
interviewer pushes back. Each drill has up to five steps:

1. **Make the call**: state your choice and the one reason it wins.
2. **Defend the why**: why that and not the alternative?
3. **Name the trade-off**: what you give up, and when your choice would flip.
4. **A new constraint**: the requirements change. Does your design hold?
5. **Something broke**: an incident. How do you find and fix it?

How to run one:

1. Open **Trade-off drills**. The recommended drill for your job is at the
   top. **Start this drill**.
2. For each step, answer out loud first, then type the gist. Open **What the
   interviewer listens for** if you're stuck.
3. **Get feedback**, or **Show strong answer** to compare. Rate yourself if
   you disagree with the score.
4. At the end you see a score per step, your weakest step and **Work on: …**
   with one tip. A highlighted **Next step** card takes you to step 3 of the
   path, or **Run it again** to beat your score.

Good to know:

- **Save & exit** keeps your place. **Finish now** scores what you've done.
  An **Unfinished drill** card lets you **Continue** or **Discard** later.
- **All drills** lists every scenario, filtered by role. Filter by **Not
  started**, **Below 70%** or **Done**. **Show all roles** removes the role
  filter.
- **Write one for this exact job** (with a pass) creates a drill from your job
  description. It's saved, so you can run it as often as you like.
- On the free plan, library drills are a **Free preview**. A pass opens the
  full library.

## 3. Your stories

STAR stories (situation, task, action, result) for behavioral rounds,
matched to what each job asks for.

1. Open **Your stories**. **Coverage** shows what this job is likely to
   probe, for example technical depth, leadership, customer focus, delivery
   under pressure, conflict and failure, and which ones have no story yet.
2. Too many gaps to write? **Draft stories from my resume** (under the
   coverage list) drafts up to 4 stories for the competencies with none, in
   one go, from your saved resume. One story can cover two. Each is marked
   **Draft · check it** until you open it, check it, fill in anything in
   [brackets] and save. It uses 1 story AI credit.
3. Or choose **Add a story** next to a gap, or:
   - **+ Quick capture**: three boxes: what happened, what you did, how it
     ended.
   - **New full story**: situation, task, action, result and metrics.
   - **Draft it for me** (at the top of a new story): write a few words about
     something you did, or tap a line from your resume, and choose **Draft my
     story**. The AI fills in the title, situation, task, action, result and
     metrics for you to check. Anything it can't know, like a number, is left
     in **[brackets]** for you to fill in: it never makes up facts. It uses 1
     story AI credit.
4. Give it a title, pick its **competencies**, and tick the jobs it
   **Suits**. Only one of title, what happened or what you did is needed to
   save: the rest is optional.
5. The **STAR check** shows what's missing, for example "Add the situation".
   **Save story**.

Then:

- **Coach this story with AI** suggests how to tighten it, without inventing
  anything.
- **Practice this story** opens it as a question to answer out loud.
- **Export Markdown** downloads all your stories.

Stories are saved on this device and synced to your account while you're
signed in. A story you're writing is kept as a draft if you leave the page.

## The question bank (any time)

Quick reps on any topic, not tied to one job.

- **Your next session**: one tap for 10 questions, starting with your weakest
  topics once you have scores. Choose **Start**.
- **Topics**: pick a topic, with your score for each one.
- **Custom session**: mode, track, topic and length.

| Mode | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Practice** | Answer, get AI feedback or see the model answer, then rate yourself |
| **Flashcards** | Quick recall: flip, then **Again**, **Good** or **Easy** |
| **Timed exam** | 90 seconds a question, no peeking, review at the end |
| **Weak areas** | Starts with the topics you've scored lowest on |

**End session** stops at any point and saves what you did.

## How answers are graded

Each answer is scored against a rubric for its type of question (system
design, data pipelines, behavioral and so on). For every criterion you see:

- a rating, from missing to strong;
- **a quote from your answer** as the evidence (no quote, no credit);
- a short note on what would make it stronger.

Very short answers, and answers missing the key criterion (for example the
trade-off in a design question), are capped, so a high score means something.
Each result also has a **staff upgrade**: the one thing that would lift the
answer a level.

## Try again and compare

After feedback, choose **Try again and compare**. The new score and every
criterion that changed are shown side by side, for example "42% → 61%" and
"Vague → Solid". **Save this version** keeps your best answer for this job
under **Your answers** in the AI Interview Coach.

## Common questions

??? question "Which step should I start with?"
    Step 1, Questions for this job, unless you know what you need. If your
    interview is behavioral only, go straight to step 3. If it's soon, do a
    mock interview first to find your weak spots.

??? question "Does practice count toward readiness?"
    Yes. Practice is 30% of your [readiness score](readiness.md) for the job
    you practised for. Make sure the right job is selected at the top.

??? question "I practised but the step has no tick"
    Ticks need the goal for that step: 5 answered questions for this job, one
    finished drill, one story suited to this job, or one finished mock. Check
    that the same job is selected.

??? question "Do I need AI feedback for every answer?"
    No. In the question bank you can show the model answer and rate yourself,
    which doesn't use your AI feedback allowance.

??? question "Generating questions failed"
    Try again: a second attempt usually works. If you've reached your monthly
    limit, the message says so. See [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md).

??? question "Can I practise without a job?"
    Yes. The question bank and the library drills work without one. The voice
    mock has a **General practice (no job)** option.
