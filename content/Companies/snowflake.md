## The process, as Snowflake describes it

**Most roles:** apply, then phone screens and onsite or video interviews (some teams add
steps), then a decision, with reference and background checks where the law allows.

**Engineering roles** (about **2–4 weeks**):

1. **Initial screen**, 30 minutes, with a recruiter and/or hiring manager.
2. **Technical interviews**, 60 minutes each: coding and/or system design, including live coding.
3. **Panel**, **3–5 interviews of 60 minutes**: technical, expertise, system design,
   behavioral and collaboration. A **30-minute tech talk** may be required depending on level.
4. **Decision**, usually within a few days of the final round.

Snowflake notes engineering candidates may need to attend at least one interview in person.

## What they look for

Snowflake says it wants people who **think big, push boundaries and build with purpose**,
and the panel covers technical depth, system design, behavior and collaboration.

## How to prepare

- Snowflake publishes its own prep material (a blog on the process, guidance on when and
  how to use AI in its interviews, and "Cracking the Code" videos) linked from its hiring page.
- For engineering roles, practise **live coding** and **system design** out loud.
- If your level needs a **tech talk**, prepare 30 minutes on a project you led: problem, design, trade-offs, results.

## Practice questions (written by OfferReady)

1. **Technical (Snowflake):** A query on a large table got slow after months of inserts. How do micro-partitions and clustering affect it, and what would you check?
2. **Technical (Snowflake):** When would you scale a warehouse **up** versus **out** (multi-cluster)?
3. **Technical (data):** Design a pipeline that loads JSON events every few minutes and serves hourly reports. What would you use, and why?
4. **Coding:** Given a log of user sessions with start and end times, return the peak number of concurrent users.
5. **System design:** Design a usage-based billing system for a cloud data platform. How do you make sure customers are never double-charged?
6. **Behavioral (collaboration):** Tell me about a time you resolved a technical disagreement between two teams.
7. **"Think big":** Tell me about a project where you pushed the scope further than you were asked.
8. **Tech talk:** In 5 minutes, explain one system you built: the problem, one hard trade-off, the result.
