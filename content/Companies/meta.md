# Meta interview guide

!!! info "Sources and how to read this page"
    Process and criteria: Meta's careers site and blog (sources at the bottom, checked
    October 28, 2026). This describes Meta's **software engineering** process; other
    roles differ. Practice questions: written by OfferReady. OfferReady isn't affiliated with Meta.

## The process, as Meta describes it (software engineering)

1. **Initial technical screen** (video or in person): about 5 minutes of introductions,
   **35 minutes of coding** (typically two problems on data structures and algorithms),
   and 5 minutes for your questions. Meta says it avoids trick questions.
2. **Full loop**, made of 45-minute interviews:
    - **Coding:** harder than the screen, same structure.
    - **Design:** systems design or product design, matched to your background; mostly discussion and drawing.
    - **Behavioral:** your past work, motivations and how you'd do in a fast-moving team.

## What they look for

- Your **approach** to a problem, which Meta says can matter as much as the answer.
- **Communication**: thinking out loud, explaining your reasoning.
- **Design judgement**: requirements, trade-offs, scale and failure.
- **Ownership, teamwork** and how you learn from failure.
- Fit with Meta's values (as listed on its careers blog): *Move fast; Focus on long-term
  impact; Build awesome things; Live in the future; Be direct and respect your colleagues;
  Meta, Metamates, me.*

## How to prepare (Meta's advice)

- **Coding:** practise in a plain editor with no autocomplete, and on paper or a
  whiteboard; under time pressure; in the language you know best. Get a working
  solution first, then optimise. Find your own bugs.
- **Design:** review systems you've built; practise designing large systems you use
  daily; start from requirements; spend time on the hard parts; discuss trade-offs.
- **Behavioral:** know your resume; be honest about failures; use STAR with concrete examples.

## Practice questions (written by OfferReady)

1. **Coding:** Given a list of meeting time ranges, return the minimum number of rooms needed. Talk through complexity.
2. **Coding:** Return the k most frequent words in a stream of text. How does your answer change if the stream never ends?
3. **System design:** Design a news feed for a few hundred million users. Where do you precompute, and where do you compute on read?
4. **Product design:** Design a feature that helps people find local events. How do you measure success?
5. **Behavioral:** Tell me about a project that failed. What would you do differently?
6. **Behavioral ("Be direct"):** Tell me about a time you gave hard feedback to a peer.
7. **Behavioral ("Move fast"):** Tell me about a time you shipped something imperfect on purpose. How did you manage the risk?
