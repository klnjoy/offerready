# Anthropic interview guide

!!! info "Sources and how to read this page"
    Anthropic's careers page doesn't publish its interview stages in detail, so this page
    covers what it **does** publish: its values and its rules on AI use by candidates
    (sources at the bottom, checked October 28, 2026). Practice questions: written by
    OfferReady. OfferReady isn't affiliated with Anthropic.

## The process

Anthropic says its process is designed to find "thoughtful people with diverse
expertise", but it doesn't list stages or formats publicly. **Ask your recruiter** what
each round covers.

## Rules on using AI (Anthropic's candidate guidance)

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Refining **your own** first draft of a resume or application answers | Using AI to write about experience you don't have |
| Researching Anthropic, practising answers, preparing questions and study guides | **Live interviews: no AI help** unless they say otherwise |
| AI on a take-home only when Anthropic says it's permitted for that task | Having AI write assessment code when it isn't explicitly allowed |

Anthropic also asks candidates to be open about how they used AI. OfferReady follows
the same line: it's for practice, and the Coach never invents experience.

## What they value

Anthropic's careers page lists seven values:

1. Act for the global good
2. Hold light and shade
3. Be good to our users
4. Ignite a race to the top on safety
5. Do the simple thing that works
6. Be helpful, honest, and harmless
7. Put the mission first

Its stated mission is to ensure the world safely makes the transition through
transformative AI.

## How to prepare

- Read its research and engineering posts for the team you're applying to.
- Prepare honest stories that show judgement on **safety, simplicity and honesty**, not just speed.
- For engineering roles, practise without AI tools, as the live interview will be.

## Practice questions (written by OfferReady)

1. **"Do the simple thing that works":** Tell me about a time you chose a simpler solution over a more impressive one. What did it cost, and what did it save?
2. **"Helpful, honest, and harmless":** Tell me about a time you had to tell a user or stakeholder something they didn't want to hear.
3. **Safety:** You find a feature you shipped can be misused in a way nobody considered. What do you do in the next 24 hours?
4. **Mission:** Why do you want to work on AI safety specifically, rather than AI in general?
5. **Technical (AI):** How would you test whether a model follows a system prompt under adversarial user input?
6. **Technical (engineering):** Design a rate limiter for an API used by thousands of customers with different plans.
7. **Judgement:** Tell me about a decision where you put the team's goal ahead of your own project.
