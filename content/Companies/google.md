## How Google structures interviews

Google describes **structured interviewing**: candidates for the same role get the same
questions, scored on a common rubric, against qualifications set in advance. Questions
come with planned follow-ups, and interviewers weigh **how you reason** more than one
right answer.

Two question styles:

- **Behavioral:** "Tell me about a time…", to check what you've actually done.
- **Hypothetical:** "Imagine if…", to see how you handle a new, ambiguous problem.

## What they look for

Google's structured-interviewing guide names three areas:

| Area | What it means |
|---|---|
| Role-related knowledge | The skills and expertise the job needs |
| Problem solving | How you break down and reason through a complex problem |
| Leadership | For individual contributors, *emergent* leadership; for managers, setting direction and developing people |

## Virtual interviews: Google's rules

- Interviews run on Google Meet; some (for example systems design or UX) may use Google Drawings.
- **Using AI during the interview, or submitting work you didn't produce, leads to disqualification.**
- Keep interview questions and materials confidential.
- Have paper, a pen and a thick dark marker to show work on camera; test your setup first.

## How to prepare

- For **hypothetical** questions, practise clarifying the goal, stating assumptions, and
  comparing two options before choosing.
- For **behavioral** questions, use STAR and keep "I" at the centre.
- Practise **thinking out loud**: the reasoning is what's scored.
- For engineering roles, practise coding without autocomplete, then explain complexity and tests.

## Practice questions (written by OfferReady)

1. **Hypothetical:** Imagine your team's main dashboard shows a 20% drop in daily users overnight. What do you do in the first hour?
2. **Hypothetical:** Imagine you must cut a product's cloud cost by 30% in a quarter without hurting latency. How do you approach it?
3. **Behavioral (leadership):** Tell me about a time you led without formal authority.
4. **Behavioral:** Tell me about a time you had to learn a new area quickly to deliver.
5. **Problem solving:** How would you estimate how much storage a photo app needs next year? State your assumptions.
6. **Role knowledge (AI):** How would you evaluate whether a new LLM-based feature is better than the current one before launch?
7. **Role knowledge (data):** Design the tables for an events pipeline that must support daily and hourly reporting. What would you partition on?
8. **Behavioral:** Tell me about feedback you disagreed with. What did you do with it?
