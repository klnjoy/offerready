## The process, as Databricks describes it

1. **Recruiter call:** your background and interest in the role.
2. **Pre-onsite screen:** a hiring-manager screen, a technical assessment or a skill evaluation.
3. **Onsite loop:** usually **4–6 interviews** with team members (virtual, on Google Meet,
   unless your recruiter says otherwise).
4. **Presentation:** for some roles, especially go-to-market and executive.
5. **Reference checks, decision and offer.**

Databricks says the whole process typically takes **two to three months**, and it aims
to give feedback within 48 hours of the final interview.

## What they look for

- **Behavioral interviews** ("Tell me about a time…") on how you work, learn, collaborate,
  handle challenges and make decisions. Every candidate is assessed on the same core competencies.
- Role-specific skills, plus Databricks' **six culture principles**:

| Principle | In one line |
|---|---|
| We are customer obsessed | Customers come first |
| We raise the bar | Keep getting better, as people and as a company |
| We are truth seeking | Decide on evidence, change your mind when it changes |
| We operate from first principles | Start from fundamentals and purpose |
| We bias for action | Move with urgency: discuss, plan, execute, repeat |
| We put the company first | Do what's best for the whole company |

## How to prepare (Databricks' advice)

- Prepare examples of past challenges and achievements; tailor your resume to the role.
- Test audio, camera, screen sharing and slides beforehand.
- **Don't share confidential information** from a current or former employer, and don't use a work computer for candidate assignments.

## Practice questions (written by OfferReady)

1. **Truth seeking:** Tell me about a time data proved your own assumption wrong. What did you change?
2. **First principles:** Tell me about a problem where you ignored "how it's always done" and started from the requirements.
3. **Customer obsessed:** Tell me about a time you went deep on one customer's problem and it changed the product or plan.
4. **Bias for action:** Tell me about a time you moved fast with incomplete information.
5. **Technical (Spark):** A Spark job slows down sharply as data grows, and one task runs far longer than the others. What's likely happening and how do you fix it?
6. **Technical (Delta Lake):** How would you handle late-arriving and updated records in a daily pipeline into a Delta table?
7. **Design:** Design a lakehouse for a retailer's online and store sales with near-real-time dashboards. Which layers, and why?
8. **Field roles:** A customer's proof of concept is failing a week before the deadline. Walk me through your first day.
