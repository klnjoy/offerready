# Amazon interview guide

!!! info "Sources and how to read this page"

    checked October 28, 2026). Practice questions: written by OfferReady, not reported
    from real interviews. OfferReady isn't affiliated with Amazon.

## The process, as Amazon describes it

1. **Application.** Tailor your resume to the job description and show impact, not just duties.
2. **Initial screen** (about 30–45 minutes) with a recruiter or hiring manager: your
   background, behavioral questions tied to the Leadership Principles, role scenarios.
3. **Interview loop:** usually **4–6 interviews of 45–60 minutes**, with the hiring
   manager, team members, cross-functional partners and a **Bar Raiser**, an
   interviewer from outside the team who helps keep the hiring bar consistent.
4. **Decision.** The panel debriefs against the Leadership Principles and the role's needs.

For **technical roles** (engineers, data scientists) about half the loop is technical:
coding, system design and problem solving. Non-technical roles get more behavioral
rounds and usually one case or exercise.

## What they look for: the 16 Leadership Principles

Amazon says the Leadership Principles are the basis of its hiring decisions, and that
behavioral questions are answered with **STAR** (situation, task, action, result).

| Principle | In one line |
|---|---|
| Customer Obsession | Start from the customer and keep improving for them |
| Ownership | Take on problems you find and fix them for the long term |
| Invent and Simplify | Find simpler, smarter ways to do things |
| Are Right, A Lot | Good judgement; seek out views that challenge yours |
| Learn and Be Curious | Keep learning, dig into new areas |
| Hire and Develop the Best | Raise others' performance; mentor and coach |
| Insist on the Highest Standards | Keep raising the bar on quality |
| Think Big | Set bold direction beyond your current scope |
| Bias for Action | Move quickly when a decision is reversible |
| Frugality | Do more with less |
| Earn Trust | Be candid, admit mistakes, fix them |
| Dive Deep | Go to the root cause; stay close to the data |
| Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit | Challenge with data, then commit fully |
| Deliver Results | Hit the goals that matter despite setbacks |
| Strive to be Earth's Best Employer | Build a safer, fairer, more productive workplace |
| Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility | Weigh the wider impact of your decisions |

## How to prepare

From Amazon's own advice:

- Prepare **a STAR story for each principle**: one specific situation, told with "I",
  with a measurable result and what you learned. Aim for about **2–3 minutes** per story.
- Research the team on amazon.jobs and ask your recruiter what the loop covers.
- Prepare questions for your interviewers.

OfferReady tip: one story can cover two or three principles. Write 8–10 strong stories
(Stories in the app), then tag each with the principles it shows, so no principle is left
without a story.

## Practice questions (written by OfferReady)

Behavioral, one per principle group:

1. **Customer Obsession:** Tell me about a time you changed your plan because of what a customer or user actually needed.
2. **Ownership:** Tell me about a problem outside your job description that you took on and fixed.
3. **Invent and Simplify:** Describe a process you made simpler. What did you remove, and what was the result?
4. **Are Right, A Lot:** Tell me about a decision you got wrong. How did you find out, and what changed?
5. **Dive Deep:** Walk me through a time the data didn't match what everyone believed. How did you find the root cause?
6. **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit:** Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. What happened after the decision was made?
7. **Deliver Results:** Tell me about a deadline you hit despite a major setback. What did you trade off?
8. **Bias for Action:** Tell me about a time you made a decision without all the data. How did you limit the risk?
9. **Earn Trust:** Tell me about a mistake you made that affected others. How did you tell them?
10. **Think Big:** Describe an idea you pushed that was bigger than your role. How did you get support?

Technical (for engineering and data roles):

11. Design a service that tracks the status of millions of orders and notifies customers when it changes. Where would it fail first at peak load?
12. A nightly pipeline started finishing two hours late. How do you find out why, and what do you check first?
